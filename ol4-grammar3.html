Small Actions Can Make a Difference

🎓 Learn the grammar

Lesson 3: Verbs + infinitive / -ing form. Tap 🔊 to hear any word or sentence.

verb + to + infinitive
Hazem is planning to go to university next year. He hopes to study medicine.
  • afford, agree, arrange, choose, decide, expect, fail, help, hope, intend, learn, manage, offer, plan, promise, refuse, threaten, want, wish, would like are followed by to + infinitive.
  • Some verbs take an object first: want / expect / warn + object + to: I wanted Omar to work hard.
  • They expected me to come first.
  • Negative: put not before to: Ahmed decided not to use my tools. (not: didn't decide to use)
  • agree has other patterns: agree with a person
  • agree on a time
  • agree that + clause.

⚠️ Important note

  • After these verbs use to + infinitive, never the -ing form: I planned to spend (not spending).
  • Promise, decide, agree can also take that + clause: They decided that they won't sell the house.
  • Negative infinitive: not to + verb.
verb + -ing (gerund)
You should avoid going out late at night. She has just finished cleaning her bedroom.
  • avoid, consider, deny, dislike, enjoy, finish, imagine, include, keep, mind, practice, recommend, suggest are followed by the -ing form.
  • Negative: put not before the -ing form: He suggested not going to the park.
  • suggest / recommend / advise / ask + (that) + subject + base verb or should + base verb: I suggested that Sama join the school trip. (not: joins)

⚠️ Important note

  • After enjoy, avoid, finish, mind, deny, suggest, imagine use the -ing form, never to + infinitive.
  • With suggest / recommend + that, the verb is the base form: I suggest that he listen (not listens).
begin / start / continue / hate / like / love / prefer + to + infinitive or -ing
We continued to run / running until we got home. The wind began to blow / blowing.
  • The meaning is the same with both forms: begin, start, continue, hate, like, love, prefer.
  • After would (would like, would love, would prefer) use to + infinitive only: I'd prefer to finish my work early. (not: to finishing)
  • After starting / beginning use to + infinitive: It is starting to get colder. (not: starting getting)

⚠️ Important note

  • would like / love / prefer / continue + to + infinitive only.
  • Avoid two -ing forms together: starting to get, not starting getting.
remember / forget / stop / regret / go on / try + to + inf. or -ing (the meaning changes)
Remember to bring your homework. · I remember waking up in the middle of the night.
  • remember / forget + to + inf. = do (or not do) a task:
  • Remember to bring your homework tomorrow.
  • She forgot to buy the newspaper.
  • remember / forget + -ing = recall (or not recall) a past event:
  • I remember waking up.
  • I forgot phoning him, so I phoned him again.
  • stop + to + inf. = stop one action in order to do another:
  • Dina stopped to talk to her friend.
  • stop + -ing = finish the action:
  • They stopped talking to each other.
  • regret + -ing = feel sorry about the past:
  • I regret wasting my time.
  • regret + to + inf. = sorry to say bad news:
  • I regret to tell you that your arm is broken.
  • go on + -ing = continue the same action:
  • Rodayna went on studying physics.
  • go on + to + inf. = move to a new action:
  • she went on to study Arabic.
  • try + to + inf. = make an effort:
  • You tried to answer the question.
  • try + -ing = experiment to see the result:
  • Try taking an aspirin.

⚠️ Important note

  • Ask yourself: is it a task / purpose (to + inf.) or a past event / experiment (-ing)?
  • forget to = did not do it.
  • forget -ing = did it but cannot recall it.
  • Learn the six verbs: remember, forget, stop, regret, try, go on.
more patterns: prepositions, adjectives, how to, let / make, hear / see
It is easy for me to do the job. · Pollution leads to having health problems.
  • Preposition + -ing: adapt to, be used to, get used to, look forward to, object to, lead to, contribute to, be opposed to, take to: He is looking forward to meeting his friends. (here to is a preposition)
  • Adjective + to + inf.: happy, sad, glad, ready, willing, eager, pleased, lucky, amazed: She was ready to live in Aswan.
  • learn / teach / tell / know / show + how to + inf.: I don't know how to use this mobile. (not: know to use)
  • what / where / how / when + to + inf.: He didn't know where to go.
  • had better / would rather / let / make + base verb (no to): She would rather stay home.
  • The gardener let us cycle.
  • Passive make: She was made to write the essay again.
  • the first / second / last / next + to + inf.: Mona was the last to watch the film.
  • help + (object) + base verb or to: He helped me do / to do the job.
  • It + be + adjective + (for someone) + to + inf.: It was easy for me to do the job.
  • advise, allow, forbid, permit, encourage: + -ing with no object (We don't allow smoking)
  • + object + to + inf. (We don't allow anyone to smoke).
  • hear / see / notice / watch + object + base verb = the whole action:
  • I heard Mariam sing a song.
  • + -ing = part of the action:
  • I heard Mariam singing.
  • can't help / can't stand / It's no use / It's a waste of time (money) + -ing.
  • had better negative = had better not (never hadn't better).

⚠️ Important note

  • to can be part of the infinitive or a preposition. After a preposition use -ing: look forward to seeing, be used to working.
  • After modal verbs (can, could, may, might, must, should, will, would, needn't) use the base verb: She can speak three languages.
  • Passive of make: was made to + infinitive.
rewrite with the word in brackets
She promised that she would help me. → She promised to help me.
  • promised / refused / decided / hoped keep to + infinitive: She promised that she would help me. → She promised to help me.
  • suggested uses -ing: Let's play chess. → He suggested playing chess.
  • warned + object + not to + inf.: "Don't touch the wire," said Dad. → Dad warned me not to touch the wire.
  • allow + -ing (no object): We don't allow anyone to smoke. → We don't allow smoking.
  • stopped + -ing: He no longer eats fast food. → He stopped eating fast food.
  • stopped + to: She stopped because she wanted to answer the phone. → She stopped to answer the phone.
  • admitted / denied + -ing: The thief said he had stolen the jewellery. → The thief admitted stealing the jewellery.
  • look forward to + -ing: Yasser hopes to buy a car. → Yasser looks forward to buying a car.
  • regret + -ing: Are you sorry for not attending? → Do you regret not attending?

⚠️ Important note

  • Exam trap: a verb that looks the same can need a different form: stop to (purpose) vs stop -ing; try to vs try -ing.
  • Check whether to is a preposition.
  • look forward to + -ing.
  • used to + base verb (habit in the past) vs be used to + -ing (be familiar with).
  • Let and make take a base verb: He let us play.
  • Passive: We were made to rewrite it.
