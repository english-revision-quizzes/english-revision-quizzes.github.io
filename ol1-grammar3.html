Past Simple · Past Habits · Past Continuous

Lesson 3: Now Starts Long Ago · The Past Simple, Past Habits and the Past Continuous. Tap 🔊 to hear any word or sentence.

  • Use it for finished actions at a definite time in the past.
  • Time words: yesterday, last night/week/year, ... ago, in 2011, one day, once upon a time, the other day, in the past, in ancient times.
＋ Subject + verb-2 (verb-ed / irregular)
Ahmed visited his friends yesterday.
－ Subject + didn't + infinitive
Ahmed didn't visit his friends. (not "didn't visited")
？ Did + subject + infinitive ...?
Did you tidy your room? – Yes, I did.
？ Wh-word + did + subject + infinitive ...?
Where did you watch the match? · Why didn't you stay at a hotel?
was / were do not use did: He wasn't at school. Was he at school? Where was he?
had (= ate/took) uses did: She didn't have lunch. Did she have lunch? (not "hadn't" and not "Had she")
Object + was / were + past participle
They watched a film. → A film was watched (by them).
The bridge was built in 2010. · Their animals weren't fed.
used to + infinitive
He used to swim in the sea. · She didn't use to wear glasses. · Did he use to go?
It was his habit to + infinitive
  • would / always + past simple also show a past habit: Sama always got up early when she was a student.
  • be used to + noun / -ing = familiar with it (They were used to working in the sun).
  • get / become / grow used to + noun / -ing = become familiar (I got used to the cold weather).
  • Same meaning: used to play = no longer plays = doesn't play anymore = stopped playing.
  • I wish + past simple → an unreal wish about now: I wish I spoke French. (I don't.)
  • It is (high / about) time + subject + past simple: It is high time you did your homework.
  • I'd rather + different subject + past simple: I'd rather you didn't come now.
  • I'd rather + infinitive (same subject): I'd rather go shopping in the afternoon. Negative: I'd rather not go.
  • For a regret about the past, use the past perfect: I wish I had arrived earlier.
Subject + was / were + verb-ing
Ahmed was reading a story. · They were cooking lunch.
－ wasn't / weren't + verb-ing   ？ Was / Were + subject + verb-ing?
What was Ahmed reading? · Passive: A story was being read by Ahmed.
  • Use it for an action in progress at a definite moment in the past: At 8 o'clock I was doing my homework.
  • Two actions at the same time (both continuous): While I was reading, Ali was watching TV.
  • A long action interrupted by a short action: long = past continuous, short = past simple.
    While he was playing tennis, he broke his arm. · When I arrived, Shahd was watching a cartoon.
  • While / As / Just as + clause (or While + verb-ing): While playing tennis, he broke his arm.
  • During + noun: During the match, he broke his arm. (never "during watching")
  • On + noun / verb-ing: On my arrival, the secretary was checking emails.
Stative verbs are NOT used in the continuous: feelings (like, love, hate, prefer, want, wish), senses (hear, see, smell, taste, sound, seem), mental (know, understand, believe, remember, forget), possession (have = own, belong). Use the past simple: I heard a noise. (not "I was hearing")
Momentary verbs (arrive, break, crash, die, reach, notice) also take the past simple: My car broke down.
