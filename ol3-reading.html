Unit 3 · Truth Vs. Lies

📚 Read and write

Trust is one of the most valuable things in our lives, yet it is not always easy to know whom we can rely on. While many people act with honesty and kindness, there are also those who hide their true intentions behind a friendly smile.

In life, not everyone we meet is truly honest. Some people put on a friendly face, but are in fact "wolves in sheep's clothing". They may deceive us to gain money, favors, or private information. This can happen anywhere: at school, in the workplace, or even among people we once considered close friends.

For example, someone might act supportively just to borrow money, with no intention of returning it. Another might spread false stories to damage someone's reputation. Such behavior can leave us feeling betrayed, embarrassed, and unsure whom we can trust.

This type of dishonesty is not limited to face-to-face interactions. In the digital world, fake platforms and social media scams copy these same tactics. They make grand promises, such as free prizes, dream jobs, or fast profits, only to exploit our personal data or disappear with our money. It is like being caught in a trap you did not realize was set.

Whether in daily life or online, there are warning signs: the person or platform applies pressure to make you act quickly; the story changes when you ask for details; the promises seem too good to be true.

To protect yourself, pause before acting, ask careful questions, and confirm the facts from reliable sources. As the old saying goes: "Trust, but verify." Although dishonesty exists, it should never stop us from believing in the goodness of people. By being cautious, wise, and aware of warning signs, we can protect ourselves and still enjoy healthy, trustworthy relationships. In the end, honesty and kindness always shine brighter than lies and deception.

⚠️ Important note

  • wolves in sheep's clothing = people who seem kind but are really dangerous.
  • put on a friendly face = pretend to be friendly. too good to be true = so good it cannot be real.
  • Trust, but verify = believe, but check the facts.
  • Look for the three warning signs: pressure, a changing story, promises that are too good.

Host: Welcome back to "Life Talks." Today, we're joined by Dr. Sarah Al-Haddad, a social psychologist who's been studying how people, young and old, can be tricked or pressured into doing things they don't want to do. Welcome, Dr. Sarah.

Dr. Sarah: Thank you. It's an important topic because exploitation can happen in ways many people don't expect. We often think of scams or theft, but it can also mean pushing someone to work for free, share personal secrets, or support harmful ideas.

Host: So it's not just about money?

Dr. Sarah: Exactly. For teenagers, pressure might come from friends who want them to join in risky behavior so they can "fit in." For older people, it might be someone pretending to help with paperwork but really trying to take control of their property.

Host: That sounds worrying. How can people avoid such traps?

Dr. Sarah: First, notice your feelings. If you feel rushed, guilty, or afraid, stop and ask yourself, "Why am I feeling this way?" People who exploit others often create strong emotions so you don't think clearly. Second, get a second opinion. Talk to a friend, family member, or trusted advisor before agreeing to anything important. And third, learn to say "no" without apology. You don't owe an explanation to someone who is trying to use you.

Host: That's great advice. Do you have a final message for our listeners?

Dr. Sarah: Yes. Your time, energy, and trust are valuable. Guard them carefully. If something feels wrong, it probably is.

💡 Words to know

  • exploitation = using someone unfairly. fit in = be accepted by a group.
  • take control of = start controlling. pretend to = act as if something is true.
  • Advice: notice your feelings, get a second opinion, say no without apology.
  • You don't owe an explanation to someone = you do not have to explain.

In modern life appearances can sometimes be misleading, but they also remind us of the importance of being genuine. Social media and advertisements may show perfect images, yet they give us the chance to reflect on what really matters. Instead of comparing ourselves with edited pictures or staged lifestyles, we can use them as motivation to appreciate honesty and real achievements.

Throughout history, people have enjoyed expressing themselves through clothes, makeup, and style. This creativity can be fun and inspiring, as long as we remember that true worth does not depend on looks alone. If we combine appearance with sincerity, we send a powerful message of confidence.

Many influencers and role models today are turning towards authenticity. They share their daily struggles, simple routines, and true feelings. Their openness encourages others to value honesty more than perfection. If they do so, many will follow.

The bright side is clear: genuine appearance builds trust, respect, and stronger connections. Real smiles, acts of kindness, and sincere words create lasting impressions. Confidence grows when we celebrate who we truly are, not when we try to hide behind illusions. By choosing truth over imitation, we inspire both ourselves and others.

💡 Words to know

  • misleading = giving a wrong idea. genuine = real. staged = arranged to look natural.
  • true worth = real value. turning towards authenticity = becoming more real and honest.
  • The bright side = the good part of a situation.
  • The writer's message: be sincere; real is better than perfect.

In the past, wars were fought with soldiers, tanks, and planes. Today, a new kind of war is spreading across the world. It does not use bullets or bombs. Instead, it uses words: lies, half-truths, and rumors. Experts call this the "war of the new generation."

In this kind of war, the goal is not to destroy buildings, but to damage trust. Lies can be used to make people angry, afraid, or confused. A rumor can spread quickly through social media, making millions believe something that is not true. When people no longer know what to believe, it becomes easier to control them.

The victims of these "information attacks" can be individuals, companies, or even whole countries. A false story about a famous person can ruin their career. A rumor about a company can cause customers to stop buying its products.

One dangerous thing about this invisible weapon is how hard it is to fight back. Once a lie is shared online, it can travel faster than the truth. People may share it without checking if it is correct. Some do it because they are angry; others because they find it exciting.

Protecting ourselves from this new kind of war requires awareness. We must think before we believe or share a story. We should ask: "Where did this information come from? Is it supported by facts? Who might benefit from me believing this?" In the war of the new generation, the most powerful defense is a careful and questioning mind.

💡 Words to know

  • half-truth = a statement that is only partly true. rumor = a story that may not be true.
  • fight back = defend yourself. victim = a person who is harmed.
  • require + noun: Protection requires awareness.
  • Compare: the past (soldiers, tanks, bullets) and the new generation war (words, lies, rumors).
An essay has three parts
Introduction (first paragraph)
Body paragraphs (explain and support)
Conclusion (summarize and close)
1 · Introduction
Start with a hook: an opening sentence that catches the reader's interest, such as a strong question.
Give a little background information.
End with a clear thesis statement: the main idea or argument.
2 · Body paragraphs
Start each one with a topic sentence that introduces the main point.
Support it with facts, examples and reasons.
Use transitions (Firstly, Secondly, Moreover, For example) to connect ideas.
3 · Conclusion
Restate the thesis using different words.
End with a final thought, reflection or call to action.
Start with: In conclusion, To sum up, To conclude.

Model essay: Words can be more dangerous than weapons

Words are powerful tools that can build or destroy. Many people believe that weapons are the most dangerous things in the world, but words can sometimes cause even more harm. Words do not leave physical wounds, but they can hurt people deeply and affect entire societies.

Firstly, words can ruin a person's life. Bullying, rumors, or harsh criticism can destroy someone's confidence or reputation. A single lie or a comment can make a person feel alone or hopeless, which sometimes leads to serious problems.

Secondly, words can divide families, friends, or whole countries. Fake news and misinformation spread quickly, especially on social media. When people believe lies, they become angry or afraid. This can lead to conflict, violence, or even war.

In conclusion, words have the power to heal or to harm. We should always think before we speak or write. Using our words responsibly is as important as using any weapon safely.

✅ Why this works

  • Hook and thesis: the first paragraph says words can be more harmful than weapons.
  • Topic sentences: "Firstly, words can ruin a person's life." and "Secondly, words can divide ..."
  • Conclusion: starts with In conclusion and restates the idea in new words.
  • Aim for 120 to 150 words.
