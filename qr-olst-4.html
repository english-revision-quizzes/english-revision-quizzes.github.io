What happens 📖

  • The golden sun rises. Santiago's body is stiff, his hands are cut and his back aches, but his eyes are clear and his spirit is unbroken.
  • He feels proud that no one will see this fight, but he and the fish will know it.
  • He calls the fish his brother, but he knows he must kill it. He understands that the battle is not about hours but about days.
  • He thinks again of the baseball player who played with a painful bone spur in his heel. If that man could play with pain, so can he.
  • On the second night the fish pulls the boat faster than ever. Santiago's hands bleed, but he does not give in.
  • He feels alone and yet close to the fish, because the line joins them. The memory of the lions gives him strength.
  • The next morning the marlin leaps out of the water. It is longer than the skiff, silver in the sun, like a living sword.
  • Santiago whispers that he has never seen such a noble fish, and he knows now how big his enemy is.
  • For two days and two nights he does not sleep. He eats a little raw fish and drinks sparingly.
  • At the third dawn he is almost exhausted, but the marlin is slowing down too. It is you or me, he says.
