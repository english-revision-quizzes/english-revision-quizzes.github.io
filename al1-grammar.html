Unit 1 · Present tenses

🎓 Learn the grammar

Present tenses and state verbs from Unit 1. Tap 🔊 to hear any word or sentence.

Form
I / we / you / they + verb
They watch this programme.
he / she / it + verb + s / es / ies
She visits her aunt every Friday.
Uses
1 regular activities, routines and habits
Mum and Dad always watch this programme.
2 permanent situations (personal facts)
He doesn't enjoy big parties.
3 general truths
The sun rises in the east.
4 future events or timetables
The train leaves at 8:00 a.m. tomorrow.
5 time clauses with as soon as, when, after, before, until
I will write this letter before I go out.
Adverbs of frequency
always, usually, regularly, sometimes, occasionally, often, rarely / seldom, hardly ever, ever (question), never (negative), every (day, week ...)
I always get to school early.
Place them after to be and helping verbs, and before main verbs.
She is never late.
He hardly ever visits.
Form
am / is / are + verb + ing
I am studying my grammar lesson now.
Uses
1 activities happening at the time of speaking
She is having a shower.
2 temporary activities, for a limited period
I'm learning Chinese these days.
3 changing situations
Car prices are going higher and higher.
4 future events already arranged
I am getting married in June.
Time words
right now, at the moment, now, at present
We're having lunch at the moment.
Present simple or continuous?
Permanent situation: present simple.
Temporary: present continuous.
I live in Aswan but at the moment I'm living in Cairo.
Regular action: present simple.
Right now: present continuous.
My brother usually plays football, but today he is staying at home.
Not used in the continuous
knowing: believe, know, understand
I don't know the answer.
liking: hate, like, love, prefer
I like this food.
senses: hear, see, smell, taste
Do you hear that?
state: be, contain, include
It contains sugar.
possession: have, own, possess, want, wish
Do you want to go home?
Form
have / has + past participle
She has arrived.
Uses
1 present situations connected to past actions
I feel ill because I have eaten too much chocolate.
2 actions that started in the past and continue now
Alan has lived in London since 1990.
3 actions with a result now
Ali's car is shining.
He has just washed it.
4 past actions when the time is not important
We have visited Dubai many times.
Time words
since = the start of the period
We haven't seen Omar since 2001.
for = the length of the period
We haven't seen Omar for six years.
just = a short time ago
They've just gone out.
already = before now (surprise); at the end of the sentence for surprise
Have you finished already?
yet = at the end of negative sentences and questions
I haven't started yet.
still = surprise that something has not happened
I still haven't said sorry.
have been to / have gone to
has gone to = he is there now
He has gone to the post office.
has been to = he went and came back
He has been to the post office.
Form
have / has + been + verb-ing
We have been cleaning all morning.
Uses
1 an action that started in the past and is still going on
I have been waiting for two hours.
2 an action that has just stopped and has a result now
He's wet because he has been cycling in the rain.
3 an action started in the past, continuing, but not at the moment of speaking
I have been learning English for two years.
Perfect simple or continuous?
Simple: the result or completion.
How many / how much?
I have done ten exercises this morning.
Continuous: the duration.
How long?
I have been doing exercises for two hours.
Two groups of verbs
Activity verbs describe actions and can be continuous
eat, drink, study, play
State verbs describe states and are not used in continuous
seem, need, have, look
State verbs
Mind: admit, believe, doubt, want, mean, think, know, remember, understand, suppose, imagine, agree, realize, prefer
I believe you.
Emotions: adore, love, care, hate, like, hope, dislike
I love this house.
Having and being: possess, have, own, be, include, wish, belong, need, contain, resemble, seem, appear
We own a big flat.
Senses: feel, smell, hear, sound, look, taste, see
The cake tastes delicious.
Some verbs change meaning
think = believe (state)
think = consider (activity)
I think this is great.
I am thinking about my exam.
have = own (state)
have = eat, drink (activity)
We have a car.
She is having tea.
see = understand (state)
see = meet (activity)
I see what you mean.
I'm seeing the doctor at ten.
taste = has the flavour / taste = try
The cake tastes sweet.
I'm tasting the soup.
feel = believe (state)
feel = experience (activity)
I feel it is wrong.
We were feeling tired.
