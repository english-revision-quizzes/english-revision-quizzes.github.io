Modal verbs: should · must · have to · need to

🎓 Learn the grammar

Lesson 3: Voluntary Work · Modal verbs: should, must, have to, need to. Tap 🔊 to hear any word or sentence.

should / shouldn't + infinitive
You should work hard. · You shouldn't get up late. · Should I take a rest? · What should I do?
  • Use it to give advice or say what you think is a good idea.
  • Passive: You should revise your essay. → Your essay should be revised.

⚠️ Important note

  • After should / shouldn't use the base verb: You should go, not You should to go or should goes.
  • should is the same for I / he / she / they. Never add -s.
  • should = advice (a good idea). It is weaker than must.
must / mustn't + infinitive
Drivers must follow the traffic rules. · You mustn't smoke in hospitals.
  • must = a rule, or a strong personal feeling (I must go and buy some bread).
  • mustn't = it is forbidden, or a really bad idea.
  • Passive: The rules must be followed.

⚠️ Important note

  • mustn't means forbidden. It does NOT mean "not necessary".
  • For a rule from you or the speaker use must. For a rule from outside use have to.
  • must has no past form. In the past use had to.
I missed the bus. I should have got up earlier. · You shouldn't have stayed up so late.

⚠️ Important note

  • should have + past participle is about the past: it shows regret or blame about a mistake.
  • Look at the second verb: should have got, not should have get.
  • Compare:
  • should get = advice for now or the future.
  • should have got = a mistake in the past.
  • You ought to / ought not to + inf.
  • You had better (not) + inf.
  • It's better / advisable / desirable (not) to + inf.
  • It's inadvisable / undesirable to + inf.
  • I advise you (not) to + inf.
  • My advice to you is (not) to + inf.
  • If I were you, I'd (I wouldn't) + inf.
  • It would be a good idea (not) to + inf.

⚠️ Important note

  • After ought to, had better and advise you to use the base verb.
  • had better has no to:
  • You had better leave.
  • NOT had better to leave.
  • The negative is ought not to / had better not / advise you not to. Put not in the right place.
I have to arrive at 9 sharp. My boss is strict. · He has to pass his exams. · I had to break the door (past).
don't / doesn't have to + inf. (not necessary) · didn't have to (it was not necessary)
  • mustn't = forbidden. don't have to = not necessary, you can choose.
  • You mustn't leave now (it is forbidden). · You don't have to leave now (it is your choice).

⚠️ Important note

  • don't have to = it is not necessary.
  • mustn't = it is forbidden. Many students mix them!
  • With he / she / it say has to and doesn't have to.
  • The past of both must and have to is had to.
  • I need to go to bed earlier.
  • He needs to do something.
  • don't / doesn't need to + inf. = needn't + inf. (no "to" after needn't):
  • You needn't carry all the bags.
  • Past:
  • didn't have to + inf. = it was not necessary and I did not do it.
  • needn't have + p.p. = I did it, but it was not necessary.
  • It is necessary for him to + inf.
  • It is a necessity / a must for us to + inf.

⚠️ Important note

  • After needn't there is NO to:
  • You needn't go.
  • But after don't need there IS to: You don't need to go.
  • needn't have + p.p. = you did it, but it was not necessary.
  • didn't have to = it was not necessary, so you did not do it.
  • It is necessary / a must / a necessity are other ways to say must.
  • You aren't allowed to park here.
  • It's against the law to park here.
  • It's not permitted / banned / prohibited / forbidden / illegal to park here.
  • You are banned / prohibited / forbidden from parking here. (from + verb-ing)
Exam trap: Taking photos here is forbidden = You mustn't take photos here. With one / you / we, use must / mustn't.

⚠️ Important note

  • Exam trap: a sentence with forbidden / banned / prohibited / illegal / not allowed usually becomes mustn't.
  • After is forbidden / banned / prohibited use to + verb. After forbidden from use verb-ing.
  • When you rewrite with One / You / We, use mustn't or must. Do not change the meaning.
