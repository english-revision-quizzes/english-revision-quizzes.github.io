Countable and uncountable nouns · Quantifiers

🎓 Learn the grammar

Lesson 3: Countable and uncountable nouns, quantifiers. Tap 🔊 to hear any word or sentence.

a / an / one / every / each / this / that / these / those / some / any / many / a few / several / numbers + countable noun
I have a few books in my bag. Five nurses are helping the doctor.
  • Countable nouns can be counted:
  • one girl, two girls.
  • They have a singular and a plural form.
  • Regular plural: add -s / -es / -ies: girl - girls
  • bus - buses
  • library - libraries.
  • Irregular plurals: child - children
  • man - men
  • foot - feet
  • medium - media
  • datum - data
  • crisis - crises
  • oasis - oases
  • phenomenon - phenomena.
  • Singular words: a / an / one / every / each / this / that + singular noun: Each tourist has a camera.
  • Plural words: these / those / many / a few / several / two, three... + plural noun.

⚠️ Important note

  • Never use a / an / one with a plural noun: a few books (not a few book).
  • Every and each are followed by a singular noun and a singular verb: Every player needs to train.
uncountable noun = no plural, no a / an
I would like some water. There is a lot of information on the internet.
  • Uncountable nouns cannot be counted one by one. They have no plural form and take a singular verb.
  • Liquids:
  • water, juice, tea, coffee, oil, milk, soup.
  • Gases: oxygen, hydrogen.
  • Meals: breakfast, lunch, dinner.
  • School subjects:
  • history, maths, physics.
  • Languages: English, Arabic.
  • Activities: reading, shopping, studying.
  • Sports: football, swimming.
  • Materials:
  • gold, iron, sand, sugar, wood.
  • Natural phenomena: rain, snow, heat, thunder.
  • Abstract nouns: honesty, hope, beauty, help, love, confidence, courage, experience, progress, evidence, patience, peace.
  • Other nouns: money, furniture, equipment, luggage, baggage, jewellery, rubbish, traffic, electricity, news, information, advice, music, bread, work, clothing.

⚠️ Important note

  • No a / an / one / these / those before an uncountable noun: This equipment is necessary (not These equipment).
  • Information, advice, news, furniture, luggage, equipment are uncountable in English, even if the Arabic word can be counted.
  • Use a / an with an abstract noun only when an adjective describes it:
  • He had a good education.
  • We spent an enjoyable time.
a lot of / lots of + plural or uncountable · many + plural · much + uncountable
I have a lot of books. Have you got many books? Did you drink much coffee?
  • a lot of / lots of can go with countable and uncountable nouns, mostly in positive sentences.
  • many + plural countable noun, mostly in questions and negatives: We haven't got many chairs.
  • much + uncountable noun, mostly in questions and negatives: There isn't much sugar.
  • In positive sentences after so / very / as / too, use much or many:
  • I have so much work.
  • She has too many friends.
  • After so / as / too do not use "a lot of": too many people (not too a lot of people).

⚠️ Important note

  • Many / much are not common in short positive answers: Yes, I have a lot of books.
  • How many + plural: How many cups?
  • How much + uncountable: How much tea?
a few / few + plural · a little / little + uncountable
There are a few people at the back. I have little money, so I need to borrow some.
  • a few = some, a small number (positive).
  • few = not many, almost none (negative).
  • a little = some, a small amount (positive).
  • little = not much, almost nothing (negative).
  • a few / few go with plural countable nouns: a few apples
  • few ideas.
  • a little / little go with uncountable nouns: a little juice
  • little time.
  • fewer + plural, less + uncountable: fewer cars, less noise.

⚠️ Important note

  • Do not say a few juice or a little books. Match the quantifier with the noun type.
  • Few / little have a negative feeling (not enough).
  • A few / a little have a positive feeling (enough, some).
some (positive, offers, requests) · any (negative, questions) · no = not any
There are some apples. Is there any cheese? He has no friends.
  • some: positive statements, offers (Would you like some tea?) and requests (Can you give me some paper?).
  • any:
  • negative sentences and questions: He didn't have any juice.
  • Is there any milk?
  • hardly any = almost no:
  • There is hardly any milk left.
  • any ... at all adds emphasis: I don't have any money at all.
  • not any = no + noun:
  • He hasn't got any friends = He has got no friends.
  • With be: I am not a teacher = I am no teacher.

⚠️ Important note

  • Do not put no in a negative sentence: He has no money (not He hasn't no money).
  • some can be used in questions when you expect the answer yes (offers and requests).
a piece of / a bit of + uncountable · a pair of + plural-only noun
She gave me a piece of advice. A pair of sunglasses was on the table.
  • To count an uncountable noun use a unit: a piece of advice, a bit of news, a slice of bread, a glass of water.
  • Plural: pieces of advice, bits of information.
  • Some nouns have only a plural form: jeans, trousers, pants, shorts, scissors, glasses, pliers, clothes, people, police, cattle, congratulations.
  • Use a pair of with them: a pair of trousers
  • three pairs of scissors.
  • The verb follows the unit:
  • A pair of glasses is broken.
  • Two pairs of socks were bought.

⚠️ Important note

  • Do not say an advice or three furnitures. Say a piece of advice, three pieces of furniture.
  • Police, people, cattle, clothes are plural: The police are here.
singular subject + is / was / has / verb + s · plural subject + are / were / have / base verb
Mathematics is not easy. Ten kilometres is a long way.
  • Subjects ending in -s such as mathematics, physics, economics take a singular verb.
  • News, knowledge, money, advice, information take a singular verb: The news is good.
  • Amounts of money, distance, time are treated as one unit:
  • Three hours is a long time.
  • Ten million pounds is a huge amount.
  • Forty litres of oil fills the tank.
  • Same form in singular and plural:
  • sheep, deer, fish, aircraft, spacecraft: A deer is running.
  • Some deer are standing there.
  • the + adjective = plural people:
  • The poor are in need of help. (= Poor people are...)
  • After a pair of the verb follows the pair: A pair of shorts is on the bed.

⚠️ Important note

  • All the money he has is enough: the verb agrees with money (singular).
  • Little children means young children, not "few children".
uncountable (material, idea) = countable (one thing) · a different meaning
I don't like orange; I like green. I ate an orange.
  • Some nouns can be both. The meaning changes: iron (the metal) - an iron (a tool for clothes).
  • paper (material) - a paper (an exam paper / newspaper / essay)
  • glass (material) - a glass (a cup) - glasses (spectacles).
  • hair (all the hair) - a hair (one strand)
  • light (brightness) - a light (a lamp).
  • time (minutes, hours) - a time (an occasion)
  • experience (knowledge) - an experience (an event you lived).
  • business (trade) - a business (a company)
  • chicken (meat) - a chicken (a bird)
  • coffee - a coffee (a cup of coffee)
  • cold - a cold (an illness).

⚠️ Important note

  • Use some / any with the countable meaning in offers and requests: Would you like some help?
  • To count the uncountable meaning use a unit: a piece of paper (not a paper, if you mean the material).
